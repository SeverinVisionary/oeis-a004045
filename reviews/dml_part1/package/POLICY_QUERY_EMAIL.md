**Subject:** Pre-submission policy query: disclosed use of generative AI

Dear Editors,

I am considering submitting a four-page research article to Discrete
Mathematics Letters. In preparing it I used generative language models —
Anthropic Claude (Opus 5 and Fable 5.1), OpenAI GPT-5.6, and DeepSeek v4 —
substantially, for literature exploration, for generating candidate arguments,
and for drafting text. I independently checked the mathematical arguments, the
factual claims and the bibliographic references, made all final scholarly
decisions, and would disclose this use in the manuscript.

Would a submission prepared in this way be acceptable to the journal?

I have not attached the manuscript, as this is only a policy query.

## Why it is worded this way

- **It asks one question and stops.** Editors answer short, answerable emails.
- **It names the tools.** The current EMS code of practice recommends declaring
  which automated tools were used and how. DML's own ethics page links an EMS
  document approved in 2012 rather than the 2025 replacement, so naming is not
  demonstrably binding here — but it is the safer current practice.
- **It states the verification broadly**, covering the references and factual
  claims rather than the mathematics alone, because the tools were used for
  literature work and prose too.
- **Deliberately removed** from the earlier draft, on review: the sentence
  calling the result "an elementary theorem with a half-page proof a referee can
  check by hand", which makes the contribution sound trivial; the offer "I will
  not take up your referees' time", which hands the editor a rejection frame;
  and the inventory of every page searched for a policy, which reads as
  defensive.

## If no reply arrives

Cap the wait at about two weeks, then submit with the disclosure in place. DML
publishes nothing prohibiting this use, an editor would see the disclosure at
screening in any case, and the manuscript already satisfies the COPE position
(disclose the use, no AI authorship).
